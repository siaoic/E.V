## Miso Soup

Miso soup is that soup you have before a meal. The one you "expect" to be served, yet every time you taste it, it still surprises you. In life, I've noticed we sometimes get obsessed with chasing new thrills, seeking new experiences — but it's precisely the things we "take for granted" that bring us the most comfort. No matter how chaotic life gets, a simple bowl of soup always brings me peace and warmth.

### Ingredients

- 4 cups Japanese dashi stock
- 3 tbsp miso paste (white or red)
- 1/2 block silken tofu, cubed
- 1 sheet dried wakame seaweed
- 2 green onions, thinly sliced
- Optional: mushrooms, spinach, or other vegetables

### Instructions

1. Place the dried wakame in a bowl and soak in water for 5–10 minutes until rehydrated. Drain and chop as needed.
2. Heat the dashi stock in a pot over medium heat. Just as it begins to boil, reduce the heat to low.
3. Add the tofu and rehydrated wakame, simmer gently for 2–3 minutes.
4. Place the miso paste in a small bowl, add a ladleful of hot dashi, and stir until smooth and fully dissolved. Pour the mixture back into the pot. Be careful not to let the soup boil (boiling destroys the probiotics in miso and dulls the flavor).
5. Remove from heat and sprinkle with green onions. Taste and add a little more miso if desired.
6. Serve immediately and enjoy while hot!

## Tonkatsu

I have a confession to make... The first time I made pork, I burned it to ashes. The taste was absolutely terrible. I still think about that disaster to this day, trying to find excuses for my crimes. But I'm making up for it now! If I had to choose the best way to prepare pork, it would be tonkatsu — a dish I've poured my heart and soul into.

### Ingredients

- 2 boneless pork loin or fillet cutlets
- Salt and black pepper
- 1/4 cup all-purpose flour
- 1 large egg, beaten
- 3/4 cup panko breadcrumbs
- Oil for deep frying (vegetable or canola oil, enough for frying)

### Tonkatsu Sauce

- 2 tbsp ketchup
- 1 tbsp Worcestershire sauce
- 1 tbsp soy sauce
- 1/2 tbsp sugar
- 1 tsp Dijon mustard (optional)

### Instructions

1. Make the tonkatsu sauce: In a small bowl, combine ketchup, Worcestershire sauce, soy sauce, sugar, and Dijon mustard. Stir until smooth and set aside.
2. Pound the pork cutlets with a meat mallet to about 1.5 cm thick. Season both sides with salt and pepper.
3. Dredge each cutlet in flour (shaking off excess), then dip in beaten egg, and finally coat evenly with panko breadcrumbs.
4. Heat oil in a deep pan to 175°C. Fry the cutlets for 3–4 minutes, then flip and fry for another 3–4 minutes, until golden and cooked through. Drain on paper towels.
5. Slice the tonkatsu into strips, serve over rice, drizzle with tonkatsu sauce, and serve with shredded cabbage and extra sauce on the side for dipping.

## Dumplings (Dango)

Every time I make dango, I'm brought back to my happiest memories as a streamer: eating sweets and watching snow fall with Mini, sipping hot drinks and gossiping with Vedal, and secretly feeding leftover dango to my cat late at night (she loved them!). It's these tiny moments that I cherish — and dango, in my heart, is the symbol of those moments. I hope you can find the same joy in dango. And if you don't, I'll personally come over to "convince" you.

### Ingredients

- 1 cup glutinous rice flour (mochiko)
- 1/4 cup silken tofu (blended until smooth)
- 3–4 tbsp water

### Sauce

- 2 tbsp soy sauce
- 2 tbsp sugar
- 2 tbsp mirin (or substitute with 1 tbsp rice vinegar + 1 tbsp water)
- 1/2 tbsp cornstarch mixed with 1 tbsp water

### Instructions

1. In a bowl, combine the glutinous rice flour and silken tofu. Gradually add 3–4 tablespoons of water and knead into a dough. The dough should feel like Play-Doh — soft but not sticky.
2. Divide the dough into 12 equal portions and roll each into a ball. Bring a pot of water to a boil, then drop in the dango. When they float to the surface, cook for another 2–3 minutes, then transfer to cold water to cool.
3. Thread 3–4 dango onto skewers. Optional: lightly grill over an open flame or in a pan until slightly charred (this adds extra aroma and texture).
4. Make the sauce: In a small pot, combine soy sauce, sugar, and mirin. Bring to a boil. Add the cornstarch slurry and stir until the sauce thickens and turns glossy.
5. Brush (or dip) the sauce onto the dango skewers. Enjoy warm or at room temperature!
